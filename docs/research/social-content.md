# What competitors post on TikTok and Instagram, and what actually works

_Researched 2026-09-08/09 in a logged-in Chrome session. TikTok profile grids error on the web client, so every TikTok number comes from search results (likes, not views) and individual video pages (likes / comments / saves / shares). Instagram numbers come from public profile pages logged out (reel play counts, follower counts). The Meta Ad Library and TikTok Creative Center were unreachable (facebook.com is blocked for the browser extension), so paid creative is inferred from "Paid partnership" labels and the QUITTR founder interview, not observed directly. Feature/pricing research lives in `competitors.md` and `quittr.md`._

## 1. The short version

- **No brand account wins on its own.** Reach comes from four engines: (a) creators paid per view and tracked with promo codes (QUITTR, Brick, Reframe circa 2021), (b) a brand-owned human persona (Opal's "Olivia Unplugged", Sunflower's "Mr Impulsive"), (c) one product-as-prop gag clip (Jones, 24M views), and (d) users posting the product screen as a meme (I Am Sober, 216K likes). Brand accounts that post about the app itself sit at 1–15K views per reel (Sunflower, I Am Sober, Sunnyside).
- **TikTok search bans the words.** "quit porn app" and "quit weed app" return no results and a guidelines interstitial. Every creator writes 🌽 / "corn" / "g00n" / "lust", and hashtags are `#cornaddiction`, `#christiantok`, `#hopecore`, `#motivation`. Captions that name the category plainly do not surface.
- **Saves and shares, not likes, mark the videos that convert.** The videos with the highest save ratio are lists people come back to when tempted ("Remember this before gooning: 1)…7)" — 85.6K likes, 18K saves) and day-count vlogs whose comments fill with strangers posting their own streaks. The highest share ratio is body-proof ("current skin from vaping" — 296.5K likes, 31K shares) and "unc" lectures people send to a friend who vapes (51.7K likes, 21.2K shares).
- **The caption template is the conversion mechanism.** Every QUITTR creator caption is identical: `Quit 🌽 with @quittr.app · use code NAME`. The code is the discount, the attribution, and the ad-usage-rights hook in one line. Instagram collab posts put the creator's reel on the brand grid, which is how a 34K-follower account shows a 6.8M-view reel.
- **"Paid partnership" labelled videos underperform unlabelled creator posts** from the same campaigns by 10–30× (Paryss, labelled, 1.0–1.7K likes; Isabella Diaz, unlabelled, 41.5K on the same brief). The exception is a big-name creator's labelled post (Jeremiah Jones, 50.7K).

## 4. What converts (as opposed to what reaches)

1. **Promo code in the caption.** Discount + attribution + a reason for the creator to keep posting. QUITTR pays $3 per 1,000 views on top; the UGC Army takes 10% of ad revenue.
2. **App screen in the video, usage rights in the contract.** Organic clips become the paid ads. The brief is "show the interface", not "review the app".
3. **Instagram collab posts.** The creator's reel is co-authored with the brand, so it appears on the brand grid and inherits the brand's followers as a second audience. Brick and QUITTR both do this; their own reels are 10–100× smaller.
4. **Unlabelled beats labelled.** Same creator, same brief: Isabella Diaz unlabelled 41.5K vs labelled 553–9K. (Disclosure rules apply; the lesson is that the creator's own voice, not a scripted ad read, is what performs.)
5. **Search-intent phrases.** TikTok autocompletes "Is X free or paid?", "X reviews", "what happened to X", "X vs Y". Nobody in the category answers them on-platform except third-party DIY creators.
6. **The quiz-to-hard-paywall funnel** (QUITTR: 99% quiz completion, ~25% paid) is what the traffic lands on; the content only has to get the install.

## 5. What this means for Curb

Curb is multi-addiction, flat and calm, and makes no privacy claims. The competitive content field is single-addiction, loud, and euphemism-bound. The gaps:

1. **Own "one app for all of it".** Every competitor's content is one substance. Curb's native story is the person who quit vaping and is now doing the same with scrolling, or drinking. Format 1 (day-count vlog) across two habits at once is unclaimed.
2. **Build a brand-owned persona before paying creators.** One face, one recurring set (Opal's whiteboard), research-backed, 3 posts a week, bio "Powered by Curb". Cheapest reach in the study and reusable across every category. Sunflower's version (Mr Impulsive) proves the floor is low (100–1.5K) if the videos are aphorisms; Opal's proves the ceiling (2.6M) if they answer a question people actually have ("How to stop feeling tired all the time").
3. **Run a Reframe-style science-card series on Instagram** for every habit Curb supports: one fear-fact headline, numbered body explanation, real numbers ("testosterone suppressed up to 72 hours"). Fits Curb's flat visual system, needs no faces, and Reframe shows it compounds to 350K followers.
4. **Seed 20 micro-creators with codes, on TikTok, on the QUITTR brief but the Curb voice**: raw selfie, day count, app screen on camera, code in caption, collab-post on Instagram. Pay per 1,000 views. Start in the porn niche only if willing to write 🌽 in every caption; otherwise start with vaping (creators there are cheaper, the skin/energy hook is proven, and search isn't banned).
5. **Make the product screenshot-worthy.** I Am Sober's 216K-like meme is about a notification. Curb already charts every urge by the hour; make the streak card, the urge chart and the milestone notification things a user wants to post, and give them a share sheet. Watch `#curbapp` for organic screens.
6. **Answer search intent on-platform**: "Is Curb free?", "Curb vs QUITTR", "Curb vs I Am Sober", "best app to quit vaping and scrolling". Third-party DIY creators get 12–25K on these for Brick.
7. **Write to partners too.** Promise's 114K video is the girlfriend's phone. A Curb accountability-partner feature (see `competitors.md`, Nomo/Sober Grid) would give partners a reason to post.
8. **Don't build on the anti-pattern.** No feature demos, no podcast clips, no "download now" brand voice. Sunnyside has 97K followers and 500-play reels.
